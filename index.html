The premise

Every firm has the same models. The edge is in your context.

  • Frontier models are a commodity

    Any fund in Dubai, London or Singapore can sign up for the same model tomorrow, so the model alone can't give you an advantage. What sets you apart is what it knows about how your firm invests.

  • Point tools fragment your knowledge

    One vendor screens deals, another drafts memos and a third does portfolio reporting. Each keeps its own silo and its own idea of your criteria. Over time your process drifts towards whatever the vendors happen to support.

  • The Gulf is an afterthought elsewhere

    Arabic filings, Sharia screens, UAE data-protection rules and a Saturday–Sunday week rarely make a vendor's roadmap. For firms here they're part of daily work.

What it does

Workflows your team already runs, done faster and to your standard.

Origination

Thesis-driven screening

Score every inbound teaser and CIM against your own criteria rather than a vendor's generic model. The output is a ranked list with the reasoning shown.

Decision

Bilingual IC memos

First drafts in your template, in English, Arabic or both, with every figure linked back to its source document.

Challenge

The Majlis Test

A structured adversarial review takes a thesis apart, one load-bearing assumption at a time, before your committee sees it. Each thesis comes back as Holds or Sinks.

Compliance

Sharia screening

Screens apply your board's own methodology, such as AAOIFI-aligned ratios, to every holding and target. Anything flagged goes to your Sharia Supervisory Board. The tool never makes that call.

Portfolio

Monitoring & LP reporting

It assembles portfolio company packs, flags variance against plan and drafts quarterly letters. Your team edits a draft instead of building it from scratch.

Sources

Arabic document intelligence

It reads ADX and DFM disclosures, land-department records, court filings and Arabic-language press, then summarises and cross-references them alongside your English sources.
