UAE climate rules · Last reviewed November 2026

The UAE climate rulebook, on one page.

A plain-language map of the climate rules most likely to affect organisations operating in the UAE, and what each one asks for. Use it to orient yourself, then confirm the details with the issuing authority and your advisers.

Economy-wide

Rules that apply across sectors.

RuleWhat it asks forHow Nokhada helps
Federal Climate Law
Federal Decree-Law No. 11 of 2024 on the Reduction of Climate Change Effects. In force since 30 May 2025.		 Public and private entities, in free zones as well as onshore, must measure and report their greenhouse-gas emissions, keep records and take reduction measures. The ministry and local authorities set the detailed requirements, and fines for breaches have been reported at AED 50,000 to AED 2 million. Inventory built from evidence, recordkeeping in your repository and reduction plans drafted from your data
National Register for Carbon Credits (NRCC)
Cabinet Resolution No. 67 of 2024, run by the Ministry of Climate Change and Environment (MOCCAE)		 Registration and emissions reporting for in-scope entities, with larger emitters subject to mandatory registration. It is also the national register for carbon credits. Reporting packs, evidence indexes for verifiers and the Sieve pre-check
Emirate-level requirements Individual emirates and free zones can add their own reporting systems, thresholds and building rules. Abu Dhabi, for example, has issued its own reporting system under the federal law. Methodology files that capture each jurisdiction's rules side by side
Financial sector

For banks, insurers and investment firms.

The UAE Sustainable Finance Working Group has issued principles that the CBUAE, the Capital Market Authority, the DFSA and the FSRA apply to the firms they license.

PrinciplesWhat they ask forHow Nokhada helps
Climate-related financial risks
Launched November 2023		 Board oversight, integration of physical and transition risk into strategy and risk management, and disclosures consistent with internal practice Financed emissions, physical- and transition-risk screening, board packs
Sustainability-related disclosures
Issued June 2024		 Sustainability and climate disclosures by regulated financial entities Bilingual disclosures drafted from governed data, aligned with the ISSB standards where you choose them
Climate transition planning
Issued February 2026		 Guidance for financial entities developing transition plans Transition-plan drafting with stated assumptions and evidence
Cross-border

Rules from outside the UAE that reach in.

RuleWho it affectsHow Nokhada helps
EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM)
Definitive period from 1 January 2026		 Exporters of covered goods to the EU, including aluminium, iron and steel, cement and fertilisers. EU importers must account for the emissions embedded in those goods and need data from their suppliers. Embedded-emissions calculations by installation and product, plus supporting evidence for importers
Customer & investor requirements Multinational customers, lenders and investors request Scope 1, 2 and 3 data, often under their own home-country rules. Questionnaire answers drawn from one governed inventory

This page is a general orientation, not legal or regulatory advice. Climate rules in the UAE are developing quickly: deadlines, thresholds and penalties are set out in implementing regulations and authority guidance, and they change. Always confirm current requirements with MOCCAE, your local authority, your regulator and your advisers.
