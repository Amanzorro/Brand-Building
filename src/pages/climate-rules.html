Rules for all sectors

RuleWhat it asks forSource
Federal Climate Law
Federal Decree-Law No. 11 of 2024 on the Reduction of Climate Change Effects. In force since 30 May 2025.		 Public and private entities, onshore and in free zones, must measure and report their greenhouse-gas emissions, keep records and take reduction measures. The ministry and local authorities set the detailed requirements. Fines of AED 50,000 to AED 2 million have been reported. [source to confirm]
National Register for Carbon Credits (NRCC)
Cabinet Resolution No. 67 of 2024. Run by the Ministry of Climate Change and Environment (MOCCAE).		 Registration and emissions reporting for in-scope entities. Large emitters must register. It is also the national register for carbon credits. [source to confirm]
Emirate-level requirements Emirates and free zones can add their own reporting systems, thresholds and building rules. Abu Dhabi has issued its own reporting system under the federal law. [source to confirm]

Rules for financial firms

The UAE Sustainable Finance Working Group issues principles that the CBUAE, the Capital Market Authority, the DFSA and the FSRA apply to the firms they license.

PrinciplesWhat they ask forSource
Climate-related financial risks
November 2023		 Board oversight, physical and transition risk built into strategy and risk management, and disclosures that match internal practice. [source to confirm]
Sustainability-related disclosures
June 2024		 Sustainability and climate disclosures by regulated financial entities. [source to confirm]
Climate transition planning
February 2026		 Guidance for financial entities writing transition plans. [source to confirm]

Rules from outside the UAE

RuleWho it affectsSource
EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM)
Definitive period from 1 January 2026		 Exporters of aluminium, iron and steel, cement, fertilisers, hydrogen and electricity to the EU. EU importers must account for the emissions embedded in these goods and need data from their suppliers. [source to confirm]
